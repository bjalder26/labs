Evaluating the Cost-Effectiveness of Antacids Lab

Name:

Objectives:

In this experiment, two commercially available antacids will be evaluated and compared by:
  • Using back titration to determine the number of moles of H+ neutralized per gram of each antacid.
  • Calculating the cost per equivalent of acid neutralized by each antacid.
  • Determining which antacid is the most cost-effective.

Background:

Acid indigestion is a common ailment caused by the overproduction of stomach acid (HCl). Over-the-counter antacids provide some relief from the symptoms of acid indigestion. They are generally made up of some mixture of weak bases such as $\ce{Mg(OH)2}$, $\ce{Al(OH)3}$, and $\ce{CaCO3}$ that can react with HCl as shown in these net ionic equations:

$\ce{H+(aq) + OH^{−}(aq)⟶H2O(l)}$ (1)
$\ce{2H+(aq) + CO3^{2-}(aq) -> H2O(l) + CO2(g)}$ (2)

In this experiment, back titration will be used to determine the acid-neutralizing capacity of each antacid. A known amount of standardized HCl is added to the antacid in excess, meaning that more HCl is added than the antacid can neutralize. The antacid neutralizes part of the HCl, leaving some HCl unreacted.

The unreacted HCl is then titrated with standardized NaOH. Because HCl and NaOH react in a 1:1 mole ratio, the moles of NaOH required to reach the endpoint equal the moles of excess HCl remaining after the antacid reaction. The moles of H+ neutralized by the antacid are therefore calculated by subtracting the moles of NaOH used from the total moles of HCl added.

Bromophenol blue is used to detect the endpoint. The solution is yellow while excess HCl is present and changes toward blue as the remaining HCl is neutralized. The endpoint is reached when the addition of one or two drops of NaOH produces a blue color that remains after mixing for at least 20 seconds.

moles of H+ neutralized by antacid = total moles of HCl added − total moles of NaOH added (3)


Because an antacid may contain more than one active ingredient, this experiment measures the total amount of H+ neutralized rather than the amount of each individual antacid ingredient. Acid-neutralizing capacity is expressed in equivalents. In this experiment, one equivalent of antacid neutralizes one mole of H+.

equivalents of antacid = moles of H+ neutralized (4)

The more cost-effective antacid is the one with the lower cost per equivalent of acid neutralized.

Materials and Equipment

  • Antacid tablets – Rolaids®, Tums®, etc. It is preferable to use tablets that are white and have no added flavor.
  • Mortar and pestle
  • Two 250 mL Erlenmeyer flasks and at least two 100 mL beakers
  • Bunsen burner
  • Ring stand with ring clamp and wire gauze
  • Standardized HCl solution – approximately 0.2 M
  • Standardized NaOH solution – approximately 0.2 M
  • Bromophenol blue indicator solution
  • Two burets
  • Labels

Safety

  • Wear goggles at all times during this experiment.
  • Take care in handling both HCl and NaOH. Should either of these solutions come in contact with skin or eyes, rinse thoroughly with water.
  • Use caution when heating the antacid and HCl mixture. Allow the solution and glassware to cool to room temperature before handling the flask or beginning the titration.

Part A: Preparation of Antacid Sample

  1. Choose an antacid and record its name in Table 1. With a mortar and pestle, crush one tablet of antacid to as fine a powder as possible.
  2. Weigh 0.300–0.400 g of the powdered antacid into a preweighed 250 mL Erlenmeyer flask. Record the mass of the antacid sample to the nearest 0.001 g.

Part B: Preparation of Burets

  1. One buret will be filled with standardized HCl and the other with standardized NaOH. Label the burets accordingly and record the concentration of each solution as indicated on its label.
  2. It is important that the acid and base solutions are not diluted by water remaining in the burets. Before filling each buret, rinse it with at least two 4 mL portions of the solution that will be placed in that buret.
  3. Using a beaker, fill the burets with HCl and NaOH (respectively). Note that bubbles take up space (volume), so make sure to dislodge any bubbles that might be stuck in the tip of the buret by lightly tapping the tip or by letting a portion of the solution run rapidly through the tip. Record the initial buret readings for both HCl and NaOH.

Part C: Reaction of the Antacid with Excess HCl

  1. Record the initial HCl buret reading to 0.01 mL. Add approximately 20 mL of standardized HCl to the antacid sample. Record the final HCl buret reading to 0.01 mL. The exact volume delivered will be calculated from the initial and final buret readings.
  2. Gently boil the antacid and HCl mixture for approximately two minutes. Heating helps the antacid react completely with the HCl and helps remove dissolved $\ce{CO2}$ produced by carbonate-containing antacids. Allow the mixture to cool to room temperature before continuing. Some insoluble binders, coatings, or other inactive ingredients may remain in the flask. Do not filter the mixture.
  3. Add 6–8 drops of bromophenol blue indicator. The solution should be yellow, indicating that HCl remains in excess. If the solution is green or blue, add a measured additional portion of standardized HCl until the solution becomes yellow. Record the initial and final HCl buret readings in Table 3. All HCl added must be included in the final calculation.

Part D: Back Titration of Excess HCl with NaOH

  1. Record the initial NaOH buret reading to 0.01 mL. Begin adding standardized NaOH while continuously swirling the flask. NaOH may be added relatively quickly while the solution remains yellow. As the solution begins to change from yellow toward green, reduce the rate of addition.
  2. Near the endpoint, add NaOH one drop at a time while swirling after each addition. The endpoint is reached when one or two drops produce a blue color that remains after mixing for at least 20 seconds. Record the final NaOH buret reading to 0.01 mL.

Part E: Correction of an Overshot Endpoint, if Needed

If the solution becomes intensely blue, too much NaOH has been added and the endpoint has been exceeded. Add standardized HCl in small, measured portions until the solution becomes yellow. Record the initial and final HCl buret readings in Table 5. Then add NaOH dropwise while swirling until the solution reaches the blue endpoint described in Part D.

After the corrected endpoint is reached, record the final NaOH buret reading in Table 4. The volume recorded in Table 4 must include all NaOH added, including the NaOH added after the endpoint correction. Do not refill the NaOH buret during this correction.

Part F: Repeat

Repeat Parts A–E with another brand of antacid. Use the same endpoint criterion for both antacids: the first blue color that remains after mixing for at least 20 seconds.
